# Dog breed guides

## Beagle
Scent-driven and food-motivated, so Beagles gain weight easily; measure every meal. They need 60 or more minutes of exercise daily and a secure leash or fence because they follow scents. Clean the floppy ears weekly and watch for ear infections.

## Labrador Retriever
Energetic and always hungry, so portion control matters. They need 60 to 90 minutes of exercise a day, ideally with fetch or swimming. Labs are prone to hip and elbow problems and obesity, so keep them lean.

## Golden Retriever
Friendly and high-energy, needing 60 or more minutes of exercise daily. The thick double coat needs brushing several times a week, and more during shedding. Prone to hip dysplasia, skin allergies and some cancers, so keep up regular vet checks.

## German Shepherd
An intelligent working breed that needs one to two hours of exercise plus mental work such as training. Heavy shedder, so brush several times a week. Prone to hip and elbow dysplasia and bloat, so feed smaller meals and avoid hard exercise right after eating.

## Bulldog
Low-energy and best with short, gentle walks. Their flat faces make them sensitive to heat and prone to breathing problems, so avoid exercise in hot weather. Clean skin folds regularly to prevent infections.

## French Bulldog
Flat-faced, so avoid heat and strenuous exercise; short gentle walks are best. Clean the face folds regularly. Prone to breathing problems, back problems and weight gain.

## Poodle
Smart and active, needing about 60 minutes of exercise. The coat sheds little but mats easily: brush often and book professional grooming every 4 to 6 weeks. Prone to ear infections and dental disease.

## Chihuahua
Tiny, needing about 30 minutes of exercise a day. Dental disease is common, so brush teeth regularly. Sensitive to cold and prone to slipping kneecaps. Feed small frequent meals, especially for puppies, to avoid low blood sugar.

## Dachshund
The long back makes Dachshunds prone to spinal disc disease. Avoid jumping on and off furniture, use ramps, and keep them lean. They need 30 to 60 minutes of walking a day.

## Husky (Siberian)
Very high energy, needing one to two hours of exercise a day. The thick double coat sheds heavily each season and makes them sensitive to heat. They are escape artists, so use secure fencing.

## Border Collie
Extremely energetic and intelligent, needing two hours or more of exercise plus mental tasks. A bored Border Collie may become destructive. Prone to hip dysplasia and some eye problems.

## Pug
Flat-faced and heat-sensitive, so keep walks short, about 30 minutes, and avoid hot weather. Clean face wrinkles regularly. Prone to obesity, breathing problems and eye problems.

## Corgi (Pembroke Welsh)
An active herding dog needing 45 to 60 minutes of exercise. Sheds heavily. The long back means avoiding jumping from heights, and Corgis gain weight easily, so measure meals.

## Shih Tzu
Needs about 30 minutes of walking a day. The long coat needs daily brushing or a short clip. Clean around the eyes daily, brush teeth, and avoid heat.

## Yorkshire Terrier
Small, needing about 30 minutes of exercise. The silky coat needs daily brushing. Dental disease is common, so brush teeth and feed small meals.

## Boxer
High-energy, needing one to two hours of exercise. Short coat needs weekly brushing. Prone to heart conditions and some cancers, so keep regular vet checks.

## Rottweiler
Strong and needs one to two hours of exercise, plus training and socialization. Prone to hip dysplasia, obesity and bloat, so keep them lean and feed smaller meals.

## Great Dane
A giant breed with moderate exercise needs. At high risk of bloat, so feed smaller meals and avoid exercise around mealtimes. Puppies should not over-exercise while their joints grow. Lifespan is shorter, around 7 to 10 years.

## Cavalier King Charles Spaniel
Gentle, needing 30 to 60 minutes of exercise. Prone to heart valve disease, so regular heart checks matter. Check and clean the ears regularly.

## Pomeranian
Small, needing about 30 minutes of exercise. The double coat needs brushing several times a week. Prone to dental disease, slipping kneecaps and tracheal collapse, so use a harness instead of a collar.
