# Cat breed guides

## Persian
The long coat needs daily brushing to prevent mats. Wipe the eyes daily because of tear staining. The flat face can cause breathing problems, and the breed is prone to polycystic kidney disease. Best kept indoors.

## Siamese
Vocal, social and active, so they need daily play and company. The short coat needs only weekly brushing. Prone to dental disease, so brush teeth or schedule dental checks.

## Maine Coon
A large, slow-maturing cat with a long coat that needs brushing two to three times a week. Prone to heart disease (hypertrophic cardiomyopathy) and hip dysplasia. Provide sturdy furniture and scratching posts.

## Ragdoll
Docile and gentle, with a long coat that needs brushing two to three times a week. Prone to heart disease and obesity, so measure meals and keep them indoors.

## British Shorthair
Calm and easy-going with a dense coat that needs weekly brushing. Gains weight easily, so measure portions. Can develop heart disease, so keep regular vet checks.

## Bengal
Very active and athletic, needing lots of play, climbing space and puzzle toys. The short coat needs little grooming. Prone to heart disease and some eye problems.

## Sphynx
Hairless, so skin oils build up: bathe every one to two weeks and clean the ears. Keep warm and out of strong sun. Prone to heart disease and skin problems.

## Abyssinian
Active and curious, needing lots of play and climbing. The short coat needs little grooming. Prone to dental and kidney problems and some eye conditions.

## Scottish Fold
The folded ears need regular checking and cleaning. The breed is prone to cartilage and joint disease, so watch for stiffness and see a vet if the cat is reluctant to jump.
